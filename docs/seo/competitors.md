# VIZA competitor SEO research

Date of research: 2026-11-25. Market: Google Singapore, English and Simplified Chinese.
Target site: viza.it.com (free service, AI plus human consultants, Guided and Full-service tiers, B2C and B2B).

## How this was done and limits

- SERPs were pulled with the Firecrawl search tool with location set to Singapore. This is Firecrawl's own index, not a live Google page, so rank order is indicative. Treat "position" as "appears in the top 10", not an exact rank.
- Competitor pages were fetched as public HTML without logging in, then measured by script: title, H1, H2 order, JSON-LD types, hreflang, canonical, text word count.
- Word counts are approximate and cover visible text outside nav, header and footer, so FAQ text is included. Chinese pages are counted in Chinese characters (CJK).
- Chinese-language queries return noisy results (embassy sites, VFS, Facebook, Visa Inc. card pages). Few Chinese commercial agencies show up, which is itself a finding.
- No traffic or backlink figures are given because none were measured.

## Competitor profiles

Chosen: Atlys, iVisa, VisaHQ.sg, VisaCentral (CIBT), Global Singapore, HolidaySG, WuAi Global Services (Chinese), Skyline Travel Singapore (Chinese). Short notes on Vokit, IVC Services and Chinese media follow.

### 1. Atlys (atlys.com)

- Business: online visa platform, "on time or 100% money back".
- Locale model: one locale folder per country, `/en-SG/`. The folder is the country context, not a passport. The page text answers "residents of Singapore" and "Singapore citizens". hreflang lists `en`, `en-US`, `en-AE`, `en-GB`, `en-AU`, `en-SG`, `en-SA`, `en-FR`, `en-DE`, `en-NG`, `en-PH`, `en-CA`. No Chinese version seen. Canonical is the self locale.
- URL structure: `/en-SG/visa/japan-visa`, `/en-SG/visa/uk-visa`. Tools: `/en-SG/tools/visa-requirements`, `/en-SG/tools/visa-photo-maker`, `/en-SG/passport-index`. Trust: `/en-SG/wall-of-love`, `/en-SG/transparency/refunds-policy`, `/en-SG/transparency/price-change-log`, `/en-SG/transparency/status`, `/en-SG/rejection-recovery`.
- Homepage: title "Get Visas On Time to 120+ Destinations Always! | Singapore". H1 "Apply for Visas Online to 120+ Countries - On Time or 100% money back". About 675 words. No H2 headings detected. Schema: none detected.
- Destination page (Japan, Singapore): title "Japan Visa from Singapore: Fees, Requirements, and How to Apply". H1 repeats the title. Meta description starts with "Do you need Japan visa from Singapore?".
  - H2 order: Japan Visa Requirements, Visa Fees & Processing Time, How to Apply Online, Documents Required, Frequently Asked Questions, Coming Soon, Frequently Asked Questions, How We Reviewed This Page.
  - About 1,140 words. FAQ is grouped in tabs: General Information, Eligibility & Requirements, Application Process, Status Tracking, Refunds Rejections & Reapplications, Entry & Exit Regulations.

## Patterns that rank

1. Gov, embassy and official visa-centre pages own the high-intent core terms (US, UK, Australia, Schengen, India, Vietnam). Commercial pages win where the official site is thin, confusing or only a PDF.
2. Destination plus origin pages are the main commercial pattern. Examples: "Japan Visa from Singapore" (Atlys), `/visa-to-japan/` (Global Singapore), `/japan/` (VisaHQ.sg), a visa-type page (HolidaySG).
3. Passport-specific pages exist at iVisa (`/visas/japan/china`) and VisaHQ.sg (`/visa-requirements/{passport}/{destination}/tourism`). HolidaySG puts a passport table inside one page. Chinese passport holders living in Singapore are not covered by any English site seen.
4. Typical content depth for English pages that rank: about 1,100 to 5,600 words, with most between 1,500 and 3,000. VisaHQ.sg Japan is the deepest (about 5,600). The thin ones (VisaCentral Schengen about 555) rank because of brand and the official-agent status.
5. Title patterns: "{Destination} Visa from {Origin}: Fees, Requirements, and How to Apply" (Atlys), "{Destination} Visa - Price, Requirements and Application" (VisaHQ), "{Destination} visa for {Nationality} citizens: Cost and how to apply" (iVisa). Fee, requirements and how-to-apply are the recurring modifiers. H1 repeats or shortens the title.
6. Section order is stable across sites: requirements, fees and processing time, steps to apply, documents, FAQ, then trust and related links.
7. FAQ blocks are common. FAQPage schema is used by iVisa (8 and 6 questions) and HolidaySG (18). Atlys, VisaHQ.sg and Global Singapore show FAQ content without FAQPage markup.
8. Schema in use: Product with AggregateRating and AggregateOffer (iVisa, Vokit), TravelAgency and Service with Offer (HolidaySG), BreadcrumbList (Atlys, HolidaySG, Global Singapore, iVisa), LocalBusiness (IVC), TravelAction and GovernmentPermit (WuAi). Many strong rankers have no schema at all, so markup is a differentiator, not a requirement.
9. Trust elements that recur: star rating and review count, "verified reviews" (Reviews.io at VisaHQ.sg), approval-rate claim (iVisa), money-back guarantee (Atlys), licence number (HolidaySG, Skyline), phone number (VisaCentral, Skyline), refund policy line, press logos.
10. Fee transparency is rare. HolidaySG separates "Government visa fee" from "service fee" in an at-a-glance block, and Skyline separates the embassy fee. This is the closest any competitor gets to VIZA's free-service model.
11. Other page types that win: document checklists (WuAi `/route/{id}`), step-by-step guides in blogs (IVC, KKday, Wise), fee and processing-time explainers (Wise, MoneySmart, Traveloka), news and update posts (iVisa `/news/`, VisaCentral News & Updates), tools (Atlys visa requirements checker and photo maker, VisaHQ requirement checker).
12. Social and forum content (Facebook groups, Reddit, 狮城论坛, Zhihu, Sohu) ranks for natural-language questions, especially in Chinese. These answer real questions but have no structure and no CTA.
13. Language handling: iVisa and WuAi use hreflang with a language folder or Chinese default. Atlys uses country locales in English only. VisaHQ.sg and Global Singapore are English only.

## Opportunities for VIZA

### Gaps seen

1. No commercial Chinese-language destination page for Chinese-speaking residents of Singapore ranks for the query set. Chinese results are forums, news sites and one small agency product page (Skyline). WuAi has thousands of thin route pages but no price, no reviews and no real service positioning.

3. Atlys has a Japan-from-Singapore page marked "Coming Soon", and no Singapore-specific Chinese version.

5. Schengen is dominated by gov and VFS. Commercial pages are VisaHQ.sg (about 2,800 words), VisaCentral (about 555 words) and Global Singapore (about 1,680 words). A Schengen hub that also answers Chinese-passport and PR questions has room.
6. Free service angle: no competitor says the service is free. HolidaySG shows a flat service fee and Skyline charges a service fee. A clear "VIZA service fee: free, you pay only the government fee" at-a-glance block is a differentiator and fits the competitor pattern of showing fees early.
7. Trust and E-E-A-T: Atlys shows "How We Reviewed This Page". Most others show nothing. A "Reviewed by a VIZA consultant" line with date is cheap to add.
8. Schema: many pages have none. A consistent Service, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList and Organization set gives an edge.

### Recommended page types

Priority 1:
- Destination hub per destination in both languages: `/visa/japan` and `/zh/visa/japan` style (route scheme to be decided with the dev team). 16 destinations x 2 languages.
- Schengen hub plus one page per Schengen country that VIZA supports (France and Italy first), since Schengen matters most.
- Residence-aware pages: "{Destination} visa for Chinese passport holders living in Singapore" and "{Destination} visa for Singapore PR and pass holders". These map to the real audience and the gap in iVisa and Atlys.
- Document checklist pages per destination and per applicant type (EP, S Pass, Dependant Pass, PR, Singapore citizen). WuAi proves the pattern, VIZA can add price and service.

Priority 2:
- Processing time and fee pages ("{Destination} visa fee and processing time from Singapore"), refreshed with dates.
- Process guides in Chinese: appointment booking, biometrics at the visa centre, interview preparation (US), ETA versus visa (UK, Australia).
- News and update posts when rules change (ETIAS, UK ETA, Japan eVisa, Vietnam eVisa), dated and linked to the hubs.
- Free service explainer and Guided versus Full-service comparison page.
- B2B page for companies and HR teams (VisaCentral and Global Singapore both have business sections).
- Tools: visa requirement checker, document checklist generator, photo checker (Atlys has two of these).

Priority 3:
- Rejection recovery and re-application guides (Atlys has `/rejection-recovery`).
- Reviews page per destination once real reviews exist.

### Sample destination page outline (Japan, from Singapore)

Based on the best competitor patterns: iVisa hub for section order and schema, VisaHQ.sg for depth, HolidaySG for the fee block and passport table, Atlys for the review note, WuAi for the Chinese document-list template.

URL idea (final scheme to be set by the dev team): `/visa/japan-from-singapore` and `/zh/visa/japan-from-singapore`, with reciprocal hreflang (`en-SG`, `zh-Hans-SG`, `x-default`).

Title: "Japan Visa from Singapore: Fees, Documents and How to Apply | VIZA". Chinese: "新加坡办理日本签证：费用、材料与办理流程 | VIZA".
H1: "Japan visa from Singapore". Chinese: "新加坡办理日本签证".
Target length: about 1,800 to 2,500 words per language, written natively, not machine translated.

Sections in order:
1. At a glance box: do you need a visa (depends on passport), government fee, VIZA service fee (free), typical processing time, maximum stay, date checked.
2. Do you need a visa? Table by passport: Singapore, Malaysia, China, India, Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam and other top passports, with "Apply with VIZA" links (pattern from HolidaySG).
3. Visa types for Japan from Singapore: tourist eVisa, short-term visa, business, others.
4. Requirements for Chinese passport holders living in Singapore, and for PR and pass holders (residence proof, bank statement, photo, passport validity).
5. Fees and processing time (government fee separate from VIZA service, "free").
6. How to apply with VIZA: Guided (you submit) versus Full-service (we submit), three steps, what the AI does and what the consultant checks.
7. Documents checklist by applicant type (printable, downloadable).
8. Common mistakes and rejection reasons.
9. Before you travel: arrival card, insurance, entry rules.
10. Reviews and trust: reviewer name and date ("Reviewed by a VIZA consultant"), customer reviews when available, company details.
11. FAQ (10 to 18 questions, each with a short answer), with FAQPage markup.
12. Related pages: other destinations, Schengen hub, guides, news.
13. Final CTA: start application (Guided or Full-service), WhatsApp or chat.

Schema set: Organization, WebSite, Service (with Offer showing price 0 for the VIZA fee and a note that government fees are separate), BreadcrumbList, FAQPage, Article (with datePublished and dateModified) for guides. Add AggregateRating only when real reviews exist.

Technical checks: unique title per page (Global Singapore fails here), correct `html lang` (Global Singapore uses `ru` on English pages), no leaking template variables (Vokit shows another nationality on a Singapore page), self-referencing canonical, Open Graph 1200x630 image per page, `llms.txt` (WuAi has one), Chinese pages in the sitemap with hreflang pairs.
